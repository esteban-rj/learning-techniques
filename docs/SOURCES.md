# Learning techniques and sources

[← README](../README.md) · [Guía en español](GUIA.md)

Recall is based on [USAHS: 10 study methods and tips](https://www.usa.edu/blog/study-techniques/), published January 14, 2025, and the resources linked from that article.

## What is implemented

| Practice | Implementation | Linked resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval practice | Write an answer before viewing the reference; compare and self-rate. | [Retrieval Practice](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/why-it-works) |
| Feynman technique | Read, hide the notes, explain, compare, and refine. Optional AI coaching uses the supplied notes. | [Sahil Bloom](https://www.sahilbloom.com/newsletter/the-feynman-technique) |
| Spaced practice / Leitner | Scheduled reviews using five boxes with 1, 2, 4, 9, and 14-day intervals. | [Spacing review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8759977/) · [Leitner guide](https://jessewhelan.medium.com/using-the-leitner-system-to-improve-your-study-d5edafae7f0) |

The intervals follow the article’s practical example. They are not a personalized memory model. AI coaching and self-ratings do not establish measured mastery.

## All ten techniques in the source article

1. **SQ3R:** Survey the material, ask questions, read for answers, recite from memory, then review. [Linked resource](https://www.adlit.org/in-the-classroom/strategies/sq3r-survey-question-read-recite-review).
2. **Retrieval practice:** Try to bring an answer to mind before checking the source. Compare and correct after the attempt. [Linked resource](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/why-it-works).
3. **Spaced practice:** Spread practice across days and weeks. Return to what you have learned instead of fitting it into one sitting. [Linked resource](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8759977/).
4. **PQ4R:** Preview, question, read, reflect, recite, and review: a structured way to work through a reading. [Linked resource](https://e-student.org/pq4r-study-method/).
5. **Feynman technique:** Explain a concept in everyday language, find gaps, return to the source, and refine your explanation. [Linked resource](https://www.sahilbloom.com/newsletter/the-feynman-technique).
6. **Leitner system:** Use flashcard boxes to give difficult material more frequent practice and familiar material longer intervals. [Linked resource](https://jessewhelan.medium.com/using-the-leitner-system-to-improve-your-study-d5edafae7f0).
7. **Color-coded notes:** Use a restrained color key to organize concepts and make important ideas easier to locate. [Linked resource](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6794354/).
8. **Mind mapping:** Place the topic at the center, branch into main ideas, and connect related details. [Linked resource](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7472315/).
9. **Exercise before studying:** Consider a walk or some movement as part of getting ready for a focused study session. [Linked resource](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-to-boost-memory).
10. **Study before bed:** Plan a short review before a normal night of sleep, and revisit the material the next morning. [Linked resource](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321161).

Recall offers interactive sessions for retrieval, Feynman explanations, and spaced review. The remaining techniques are explained in the in-app guide; they are not separate app workflows.
